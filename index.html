About

Michael Ra-shon Hall is Associate Professor of English at Virginia Commonwealth University (VCU) and core faculty in VCU's Media, Art, and Text PhD program. His areas of specialization include 20th century and contemporary African American literature and culture, travel and imagination, cultural histories of travel, race and recreation, narrative and visual culture, and speculative fiction.

He came to VCU in 2014 as a Postdoctoral Fellow in African American Literature, joined the faculty as Assistant Professor in 2015, and was promoted to Associate Professor in 2023. He is also affiliated faculty in the Department of African American Studies and directs dissertations in the Media, Art, and Text (MATX) program.

His first book, Freedom Beyond Confinement (Clemson University Press, 2022), traces the connection between travel and imagination in African American cultural history and letters, from the press and periodicals that guided Black travelers during Jim Crow to the fiction and nonfiction that gave those experiences lasting form. His current book project turns to picture postcards and periodical covers to recover an earlier visual history of African American travel, leisure, and hospitality.

Before VCU he spent several years in Emory University's Manuscript, Archives, and Rare Book Library, where he helped process the papers of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference and co-curated the first major exhibition drawn from them.

Freedom Beyond Confinement: Travel and Imagination in African American Cultural History and Letters

Clemson University Press, African American Literature series, 2022. Hardback ISBN 978-1-949979-70-1, ebook ISBN 978-1-949979-71-8.

Freedom Beyond Confinement examines the cultural history of African American travel and the lasting influence of travel on the imagination, above all for writers of literary fiction and nonfiction. Using the paradox of freedom and confinement to frame the ways travel represented both opportunity and restriction for African Americans, the book follows that connection from the end of Reconstruction (ca. 1877) to the present.

It reads the Black press and periodicals, which offered crucial information on travel precautions and possibilities during the Jim Crow era, alongside the understudied literary fiction that later produced some of the most compelling portrayals of the freedoms and constraints African Americans associated with travel. Those often difficult experiences of mobility supplied the raw material from which writers made images and ideas, and through which they worked out a vision of freedom. In centering fiction, the book brings literature into a field of travel writing studies where it has largely been absent.

Reviewed in The Journal of Transport History (2022), MELUS (2023), and American Literary History (2024).

Interviews and talks about the book: Liverpool University Press (January 2022), Clemson University Press (January 2022), VCU News (March 2022), With Good Reason, Virginia Humanities and NPR (July 2022), and a book talk at the VCU Humanities Research Center.

Archives and public humanities

From 2009 to 2013, alongside his doctoral work at Emory, Hall worked in the Manuscript, Archives, and Rare Book Library (MARBL) of the Robert W. Woodruff Library on the papers of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference.

  • Curatorial Associate, And the Struggle Continues: The Southern Christian Leadership Conference's Fight for Social Change, co-curated with Carol Anderson and Sarah Quigley, Schatten Gallery, Emory University, on view February 21 to December 1, 2013. The first major exhibition on SCLC, it drew on the collection's audiovisual material, printed matter, and artifacts to present the organization's activism in the decades after 1968. The opening was covered by The New York Times, the Atlanta Journal-Constitution, CNN, NPR, and Emory Magazine.
  • Manuscript Collection Processor, Papers of the Southern Christian Leadership Conference, MARBL, 2009 to 2012. Supported by a grant from the Council on Library and Information Resources, the project processed the records of SCLC's activities from 1968 to 2007 and opened them to researchers in May 2012. Hall co-authored the finding aid with Sarah Quigley, Danica Tisdale, Brenda Tindal, Rebecca Sherman, Chanel Craft, Gianluca De Fazio, and Ryan Taylor.
  • Research Assistant to Rudolph P. Byrd for The World Has Changed: Conversations with Alice Walker (2010), gathering interviews and copyright information for the first volume of Walker's collected interviews.
  • Assistant to the Associate Director, Center for Faculty Development and Excellence, Emory University, 2013 to 2014, helping to organize faculty development programs and supporting the center's self-study and external review.

Teaching

At VCU, Hall teaches African American literature, speculative fiction, and interdisciplinary courses on travel, race, and recreation, from the introductory literature survey to graduate seminars in the MATX program. His senior seminars have taken up time travel in the literary imagination, major themes in speculative fiction, and race and recreation in the United States.
